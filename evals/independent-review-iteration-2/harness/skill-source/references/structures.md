# Structures

## How to offer

Pick 2–3 structures that fit the speech type, the goal and the length. For each one, show:

- its name, and one line on why it fits
- the outline filled with this speech's content (not generic labels), with a time split

The user picks, mixes or reorders. Under every structure sits the same frame:
**What? Where? → Why? → How?** (what is happening, and where → why it matters
→ how we act).

Time budget for any structure: opening about 15%, closing 10–15%, body the rest.
The closing's time is protected (`script.md`).

## From the workshop notes

| Structure | Flow | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Proof** | Tell → justify → summarize (if over 2 min) → invite | internal asks, short persuasive talks, strong evidence |
| **Situation** | Situation → problem → solution | briefings, status-to-decision, crisis communication |
| **Benefit** | Problem → solution → benefit | sales, product, change proposals |
| **Monroe's Motivated Sequence** | Attention → problem → solution → visualize → action | persuasion the audience must feel: pitches, proposals, fundraising |
| **Story / change** | Situation → action → result / change | case studies, keynotes, team wins, occasions |

## Gap fillers by speech type

### Investor pitch

The usual order (adapt it, don't follow it mechanically): problem → solution →
why now → market → product (demo) → traction → business model → competition →
team → ask. Sequoia's template ends on the vision.

- Open with your strongest proof point (traction, team, a sharp why-now), not the company history.
- Never claim "no competitors".
- Presented deck: 10–15 slides. Guy Kawasaki's 10/20/30 rule (10 slides, 20
  minutes, 30-pt minimum font) is a good ceiling.
- The ask is the CTA: the amount, the milestones it buys, and the next step
  (a second meeting, the data room).

### Sales or partnership pitch

**Strategic narrative** (Andy Raskin): name a big shift in the world → show
there will be winners and losers → tease the promised land (the island) →
present capabilities as "magic gifts" that beat the obstacles → show evidence → ask.

### Talk or keynote

- **Sparkline** (Nancy Duarte, *Resonate*): alternate between "what is" and
  "what could be", widening the gap, and end on the "new bliss", the world once
  the audience acts.
- **Story spine** (Kenn Adams): Once upon a time… Every day… One day… Because of
  that… Because of that… Until finally… Ever since then…
- Give the talk a throughline (`message.md`). Every section must serve it.

### Internal business: proposal, update, all-hands

- **Answer first / SCQA** (Barbara Minto): situation → complication → question
  → answer, then the 3 arguments. Lead with the recommendation, not the history.
- **Status to decision**: what's done → what's next → what I need from you.
- Leaders want to know what is being done, how it is under control, and what
  they must decide. Not everything that is known.

### Occasion: toast, wedding, farewell, award

- **Toast**: who I am to them → one or two specific stories → what that says
  about them → a wish → raise the glass.
- **Farewell or tribute**: what they brought → one story that shows it → what
  stays with us → a wish.
- **Award acceptance**: brief thanks (at most 3 names) → what it means → who it
  is really for → one forward-looking line.

Here the CTA is the shared moment: the toast, the applause, the wish. Usually no slides.
