The formal limit is 40.5 seconds. Aim for 38–40 seconds in rehearsal once the short pauses are included.

## Practice plan

1. Read the speech aloud once and simplify any phrase that catches.
2. Memorize the first three sentences and the final CTA word for word.
3. Give three timed runs. Keep every run at or below 40.5 seconds without rushing.
4. Record the last run. Check that “70 percent” and “vote yes” are clear, and that the final line does not trail off.
5. Ask someone outside council work to repeat the message and the requested action.

## Top three techniques

1. **Pause after the message and the survey result.** A short beat lets each memorable line land without consuming the elevator-length time budget.
2. **Stress the decision words.** Emphasize “70 percent,” “4 December” and “vote yes”; keep the surrounding words conversational.
3. **Use lighthouse eye contact.** Hold one councillor per thought, then address the full council for the CTA. Stay planted rather than pacing during this short speech.

## Q&A preparation

1. **Numbers: How reliable is the 70 percent figure?**  
   “The figure comes from the traders’ association survey: 70 percent were in favour. I would not claim more without [NEEDS SOURCE: sample size, field dates, exact question, respondent definition, response rate and sampling method]. That is the evidence behind giving Market Street back to people on Saturdays.”

2. **Hardest question: What if shops lose trade?**  
   “I do not have outcome data and will not promise a result. Judge the pilot using [NEEDS SOURCE: baseline and pilot sales or footfall measurement plan]. The evidence supplied today is that 70 percent in the traders’ association survey were in favour.”

3. **What will the pilot cost?**  
   “The verified figure is [NEEDS SOURCE: itemised pilot cost, funding source and staffing requirement]. I will not guess at it. With that checked, my ask remains a yes vote on 4 December.”

4. **Elephant: Where will displaced traffic go?**  
   “That must be answered from [NEEDS SOURCE: traffic-diversion plan, modelling and monitoring arrangements]. I will not invent a route. The aim remains to give Market Street back to people on Saturdays.”

5. **How will deliveries and business access work?**  
   “The operating details must come from [NEEDS SOURCE: delivery, servicing and business-access plan, including hours]. They should support the Saturday pilot without unsupported promises.”

6. **How will people with limited mobility reach the street?**  
   “The answer must follow [NEEDS SOURCE: verified accessibility and mobility-access plan]. That plan should make giving Market Street back to people genuinely inclusive.”

7. **What about emergency services and public transport?**  
   “Use [NEEDS SOURCE: emergency-service access arrangements and any public-transport changes or approvals]. I will not speculate; those arrangements must support the Saturday pilot.”

8. **How will the council decide whether the pilot worked?**  
   “Use [NEEDS SOURCE: success measures, baseline, measurement owner, reporting date and post-pilot decision process]. The council should judge the three-month pilot from evidence.”

9. **Why Saturdays, and why three months?**  
   “The brief specifies both but gives no rationale. Have [NEEDS SOURCE: verified rationale for Saturday closures and the three-month duration]. The decision requested on 4 December is approval of that pilot.”

## Final close after Q&A

Local shops have already backed this idea: the traders’ association survey found 70 percent in favour. For one day each week, let people stop, meet and shop. Give Market Street back to people on Saturdays. At the 4 December session, vote yes on the three-month pilot.

## Day-of check

- Bring the survey source and the verified Q&A evidence.
- Run the opening and CTA once with a timer.
- Keep notes to keywords; do not read the body.
- Finish on “vote yes on the three-month pilot”—do not add “thank you.”
