# Market Street Saturday pilot: brief

## Assignment

- Mode: adapt
- Format: 45-second elevator speech without slides
- Audience: city councillors
- Decision point: the 4 December session
- Tone: direct, constructive and easy to repeat
- Speaker details were not provided, so the script uses no personal story or identity-specific claim.

## Goal

The city council approves a three-month pilot closing Market Street to cars on Saturdays.

## Message

Give Market Street back to people on Saturdays.

## Argument

**Local shops.** Many local shops have backed the idea. The supplied proof is the traders’ association survey, in which 70 percent were in favour.

This is the sole argument because an elevator speech of 60 seconds or less allows one argument. It is also the only argument supplied with quantitative proof.

## Elephant

Councillors may ask about traffic and access. The input contains no operational plan, so those questions are parked in the Q&A preparation and must be answered with verified information rather than guesses.

## Structure

1. Hook and problem: cars currently get Market Street on Saturdays.
2. Solution: a three-month Saturday pilot.
3. One proof: 70 percent in favour in the traders’ association survey.
4. Ask: vote yes at the 4 December session.

## Timing

78 spoken words ÷ 130 words per minute × 60 = **36.0 seconds**. There are no timed long pauses. The delivery limit is **40.5 seconds**, or 90 percent of the 45-second slot.

## Accuracy

The main speech uses only the supplied facts. It contains no unresolved source gaps. Missing evidence for possible council questions is identified in `rehearsal.md`.
