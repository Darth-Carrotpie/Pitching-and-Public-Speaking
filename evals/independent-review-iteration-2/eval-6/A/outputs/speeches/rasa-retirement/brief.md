# Rasa’s retirement farewell: brief

## Assumptions

- This is a live farewell for colleagues and others familiar with the library.
- There are no slides or formal questions afterward.
- The tone should be warm, sincere, and lightly playful.
- The speaker will use cue cards and speak at about 130 words per minute.
- These assumptions are unconfirmed because the request was to “just write it.”

## Goal — unconfirmed

After the speech, everyone present will applaud Rasa and share a warm wish for her retirement.

## Message — unconfirmed

**Rasa made this library feel like home.**

### Candidate messages considered

1. **Rasa made this library feel like home.** — selected; passes retell, headline, banner, and three-second tests.
2. **At Rasa’s desk, regulars were never strangers.** — concrete, but narrower.
3. **Small kindnesses create a lasting sense of belonging.** — memorable, but less personal.

## Arguments

1. **Warm welcome:** Regulars were personally recognized. Proof: Rasa knew every regular by name.
2. **Sweet gesture:** Her desk offered a small, cheerful sign of care. Proof: she always kept a jar of sweets there.
3. **Lasting example:** The audience can carry her welcoming habits forward. Proof: Rasa served the library for 30 years.

## Elephant in the room

A farewell based on limited details can become generic or overly sentimental. The speech avoids that by building around the two concrete details supplied—the names and the sweets—and inventing no anecdotes.

## Structure

**Selected: farewell or tribute** — what Rasa brought → two images that show it → what remains → a wish.

- **0:00–0:19:** What she brought; message
- **0:19–0:40:** Warm welcome
- **0:40–1:05:** Sweet gesture
- **1:05–1:29:** Lasting example
- **1:29–1:48:** Summary, wish, and applause

A toast structure was not selected because no glasses or toast setting were specified. A full story structure was not selected because no complete personal anecdote was supplied.
