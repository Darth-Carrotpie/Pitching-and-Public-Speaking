## Podium review

The draft is clear and brief, but it sounds like a checklist rather than a Q3 story. The audience hears activity without learning the scale, significance, or business impact.

**What works**

- Friendly, direct opening
- Logical focus on Q3
- Short sentences that are easy to deliver
- Clean transition to questions

**What to improve**

1. Lead with the headline: What was the single most important Q3 result?
2. Add evidence. Replace “revenue grew,” “tickets went down,” and “customers seem happier” with specific comparisons or metrics.
3. Group the updates into two or three themes instead of listing ten disconnected facts.
4. Connect activity to outcomes. Explain what the hiring, launches, migration, and campaigns accomplished.
5. Name the main risk and the response to it. “Some risks remain” is too vague.
6. End with a takeaway or next-quarter priority before inviting questions.

Avoid listing features A through E unless every feature matters to this audience. Highlight the one or two with the greatest impact and summarize the rest.

## Stronger version

> Hi everyone, I’m Tom. Thanks for having me.
>
> Our Q3 headline is **[main result]**. Revenue grew **[X% or amount]**, driven primarily by **[cause]**.
>
> We made progress in three areas. First, we expanded the team by **[number]** people in **[key functions]**. Second, we launched five product features, including **[most important feature]**, which has already **[customer or business impact]**. Third, we completed our database migration, giving us **[reliability, speed, cost, or capacity benefit]**.
>
> On the customer side, support tickets fell **[X%]**, while **[satisfaction or retention measure]** improved from **[A]** to **[B]**. Marketing also ran six campaigns, generating **[result]**.
>
> The main risk going into Q4 is **[specific risk]**. We’re addressing it by **[action]**, and we’ll track **[measure]** closely.
>
> Overall, Q3 was about **[concise interpretation]**. In Q4, our priority is **[next objective]**. Thank you—I’m happy to take questions.

Delivery note: pause after the headline and each of the three themes. Emphasize outcomes, not the number of activities completed.
