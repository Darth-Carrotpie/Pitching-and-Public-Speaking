# Q&A

## Prepare (for the rehearsal kit)

List the 8–10 most likely questions:

- at least one per argument
- the elephant in the room, asked bluntly
- the hardest question you hope nobody asks
- the numbers question: "where does that figure come from?"

For each: an answer of 30 seconds at most that ends by bridging back to the
message, plus the data the speaker must have on hand (`[NEEDS SOURCE]` if
missing). Prepare a backup slide or appendix page for the 2–3 most technical
questions.

## In the room

- At the start, say when you'll take questions.
- **Listen, then repeat** the question, rephrasing hostile wording neutrally.
  Everyone hears it, and you gain time to think.
- If you don't understand it, ask for clarification instead of guessing.
- **Think before answering.** A short pause looks confident.
- **Answer to everyone** (the lighthouse): start with the asker, then take in the whole room.
- Thank the asker, and use the answer to reinforce your message.
- Don't take offence, don't argue, never lose your temper. Be firm but polite.
- Look for the common ground inside an objection.
- Check whether the question matters to the whole audience. If it's niche,
  offer to continue afterwards.
- Don't know? Say so, and promise a follow-up by a specific time.
- Watch the clock: Q&A eats time. End with the **final close** (message + CTA).
  The last word is yours, not the last question's.

## Spar mode (`/podium spar`)

Play the audience so the user can practise.

1. **Set up.** Take the persona from the brief, or ask: a skeptical investor, a
   CFO guarding the budget, a board member, a hostile journalist, a tired
   all-hands crowd. Defaults: tough but fair, 5 questions. If the persona and
   topic are given, don't explain the exercise: one line at most ("Skeptical CFO,
   5 questions, feedback after each answer."), then the first question. Ask for a
   brief only if the topic is missing.
2. **One question at a time**, in character. Start moderate and escalate.
   Include the elephant, a numbers challenge, and one hostile or off-topic question.
   End the turn on the question itself: no "Your answer?", no second question.
   Ground every question in what the user said. Don't invent costs or facts to
   challenge them with.
3. **After each answer**, step out of character for two lines: what worked, and
   one thing to improve (too long? repeated the question? bridged back to the
   message? defensive?). Then ask the next question.
4. **At the end**, score 1–5: composure, clarity, brevity, bridging to the
   message, handling hostility. Give the top 3 fixes, plus improved model
   answers for the two weakest replies.

Never invent facts in model answers. Use `[NEEDS SOURCE]`.
