# Rehearsal kit

The kit turns the script into a performance. Keep it to about one page: a timing
table, a practice plan, the top 3 techniques, Q&A prep and a day-of checklist.

## Timing table

| Section | Starts | Lasts | Words | Pauses | Skip if late? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Lasts = words ÷ 130 × 60 + pauses (`script.md`, "Timing"). The total must fit
90% of the slot.

## Practice plan

1. **Read it aloud once.** Mark where you stumble, and simplify those lines.
2. **The 3-second pause drill.** For at least 3 minutes (better: the whole talk),
   pause 3 seconds after every full stop and comma. You'll start using simpler
   words and shorter sentences, your intonation improves, and you breathe calmer.
3. **Three times aloud, in your own words**, without reading.
4. **Record yourself**, on video if you can. Check your words per minute (120–140
   is comfortable for listeners), the total time against the slot, filler words
   and eye contact. Paste the transcript into `/podium coach` for feedback.
5. **Rehearse in portions.** The opening on the way to work, the next part on
   the way home. Every part gets attention, not just the start, and you don't wear out.
6. **The outsider test.** For important talks, try it on a "neighbour": someone
   outside the field who will tell you straight. Can they retell the message? Did
   the analogies land?
7. **Dress rehearsal**, in conditions close to the real ones: standing, slides,
   clicker, timer.

## Self-assessment

After each recorded run, score 1–5:

- Content: **message** (clear?), **engagement** (did it pull you in?), **structure** (easy to follow?)
- Yourself: **how it felt**, **eye contact**

## Top 3 techniques

From the catalogue below, pick the 3 most useful for this speaker and this
speech, based on intake: experience, nerves, venue, format. Explain each in 1–2
lines. Mastering 3 beats skimming 20; next time, add 3 more.

**Nerves**
- **Breathing**: square (in 4, hold 4, out 4, hold 4) or triangle (in, hold, out), slow and deep.
- **Grounding, standing**: eyes closed, weight on both feet, knees soft. Feel your feet.
- **Grounding, seated**: on the front edge of the chair, back straight. Tense and
  release muscle groups one by one, from the feet up.
- **Power move and power words**: a small private gesture (pressing fingertips
  together, rolling the shoulders) and a few words you say to yourself. Recall a
  time you performed well and reached your goal.
- **Inner picture**: imagine the opening and the ending going well.
- **Reframe**: nerves feed the performance. The audience wants you to succeed.
  Nobody knows what you planned to say. Make a mistake? Keep going.
- **Mind games**: walk through the worst case (it's survivable), then focus on the
  goal. The "game of meaning": picture the audience as people actively looking
  for exactly what you're offering.

**Voice and words**
- Pause after key lines. Vary pace and tone to create impact. Speak clearly.

**Body and stage**
- **Lighthouse eye contact**: sweep the room and hold one person per thought,
  reaching every section of the room.
- **Gestures scale with the room**: the bigger the room, the bigger the gesture.
  Use your hands to illustrate words; don't fidget or touch your face.
- **Move with purpose**: know where and why you walk (e.g. one spot per
  argument). Otherwise, stand still.
- If the mic shakes, rest it against your chin.
- Don't turn to read your slides; know what's on them. If you're seated, sit
  upright near the front of the chair.
- Smile, look positive, enjoy it.

## Day-of checklist

- Drink water. Skip coffee, alcohol and milk beforehand (they affect the voice),
  and anything you haven't taken before.
- Toilet, water within reach, phone on silent.
- Tech: slides on the device plus a backup (USB, cloud or PDF), clicker,
  adapter, mic check.
- Know where you'll stand and where the timer is.
- Run the memorized opening and closing once more.
- Tell the audience at the start when you'll take questions.
